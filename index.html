OPERATIONAL · 1/5 — THE VERDICT

Every dashboard begins as one number answering one question. If this screen could keep a single element, it is this tile: cases per hour, right now. Everything added after must justify itself against it.

OPERATIONAL · 2/5 — SHAPE BEFORE JUDGMENT

A lone number invites panic or complacency. The last hour of shape says whether 47 is a plateau, a spike, or a recovery — trend is the cheapest context you can buy.

OPERATIONAL · 3/5 — THE COMMITMENT LINE

A threshold turns a chart into a monitor. The line is a promise — above it, someone acts — and only now may red enter the screen: state, never decoration. The breach tile inherits the same rule.

OPERATIONAL · 4/5 — TRIAGE + TIMESTAMP

Open load says how bad; oldest wait says how urgent; the stamp says how fresh. On a live screen, an undated number is a rumor.

OPERATIONAL · 5/5 — THE POSTURE, ASSEMBLED

Verdict → shape → commitment → context, readable left-to-right in ten seconds. Inject a surge and watch every layer agree about the same event.

TACTICAL · 1/5 — PLOT THE PROMISE

Tactical screens begin with the plan, not the progress: 120 points by day 12, drawn as the ideal line. You cannot be “behind” until behind-what is on the screen.

TACTICAL · 2/5 — THE GAP IS THE MESSAGE

Now the actual. This dashboard is the distance between two lines — everything that follows only annotates that gap.

TACTICAL · 3/5 — COMPUTE THE VERDICT

Never make the room do subtraction. The badge does the arithmetic — behind, ahead, on pace — while the chart stays underneath as its proof — and the dotted projection shows where the current pace lands.

TACTICAL · 4/5 — WHERE, AND WHY

Workstreams locate the gap; target ticks keep each bar honest; blocker age explains it. Aging work-in-progress is the most under-plotted number in delivery.

TACTICAL · 5/5 — THE POSTURE, ASSEMBLED

Promise, gap, verdict, cause. The scrubber replays the sprint — a verdict you can rewind is a verdict the team will trust.

ANALYTICAL · 1/5 — START AT THE ANOMALY

Analysis begins with the aggregate: one line, obviously bending. An analytical dashboard exists because somebody asked this line a question.

ANALYTICAL · 2/5 — DECOMPOSE

The first analytical move is always the split: one total becomes four suspects on a shared scale. Overlay beats stacking when the question is “who moved”.

ANALYTICAL · 3/5 — GIVE THE ANALYST HANDS

Filters are not chrome — they are the interrogation. Chips set scope, bars cross-filter, and every view answers to the same selection, or none can be trusted.

ANALYTICAL · 4/5 — KEEP THE ARITHMETIC HONEST

Computed readouts — total, peak, delta — restate the current scope in numbers. If a readout ever disagrees with its chart, the screen is lying somewhere.

ANALYTICAL · 5/5 — THE POSTURE, ASSEMBLED

Dense on purpose: this user brought questions. Sweep the crosshair, slice the scope — density is a courtesy to experts and an assault on everyone else — which is why this posture never ships to executives.

STRATEGIC · 1/5 — VALUE VS PROMISE

A strategic number is meaningless alone: 97 of what, toward what? The target tick makes it a kept-or-broken promise — the atom of every scorecard.

STRATEGIC · 2/5 — ENCODE “HOW GOOD IS GOOD”

Qualitative bands move judgment out of the meeting and into the pixels: poor, fair, good — agreed once, printed forever. Grayscale, so the verdict colour stays reserved.

STRATEGIC · 3/5 — DIRECTION BEATS LEVEL

The dot marks last quarter. Watch retention: the same number rising and falling are two different companies — direction is the strategic fact, so it earns its own mark.

STRATEGIC · 4/5 — MEMORY, AND THE SET

A sparkline gives each promise a year of shape in the width of a word — and a scorecard is a set: one KPI is a poster, four are a strategy.

STRATEGIC · 5/5 — THE POSTURE, ASSEMBLED

Promise, judgment, direction, memory — times four. Switch quarters: performance moves, targets hold still. That stillness is the design.

Eight rules that survive contact

P-01

One screen. The moment it scrolls, it’s a report wearing a costume.

P-02

Five-second test. Verdict first; detail on demand.

P-03

Top-left is prime. Put the answer there, not the logo.

P-04

Colour is state, not décor. Every hue means something, or nothing does.

P-05

Reserve the alarm colour. Red spent daily buys no attention in a crisis — this page banks it for alerts only.

P-06

Bullets beat gauges. Same verdict, a fifth of the pixels, six to a column.

P-07

Label the data, not the legend. The nearest label wins; make it the right one.

P-08

Date-stamp or die. Undated numbers get invented meanings.
